> [!abstract] Overview Brain injuries can damage neurons and disrupt communication between neural networks. However, the brain has some ability to reorganize surviving connections and adapt.
>
> This ability is called neuroplasticity and plays an important role in neurological rehabilitation.

## 1. What happens when the brain is injured?

The brain contains interconnected networks responsible for movement, speech, memory, sensation, and other functions.

When an injury damages a particular region, the functions associated with that region may become impaired.

For example, a person may experience:

- Difficulty speaking.
- Muscle weakness or paralysis.
- Memory problems.
- Changes in sensation.
- Problems with balance and coordination.

The symptoms depend on the location and severity of the injury.

## 2. Common types of brain injuries

### A. Stroke

!Stroke illustration

A stroke occurs when blood flow to part of the brain is interrupted or when a blood vessel ruptures.

This can cause brain cells to become damaged.

Possible consequences:

- Difficulty speaking.
- Weakness on one side of the body.
- Difficulty walking.
- Changes in sensation.
- Cognitive difficulties.

### B. Traumatic brain injury (TBI)

A traumatic brain injury occurs when an external force causes damage to the brain.

Examples:

- Falls.
- Road traffic accidents.
- Sports-related injuries.
- Physical impacts to the head.

Symptoms can range from temporary confusion to serious neurological impairment.

### C. Spinal cord injury

Although it is not technically a brain injury, spinal cord damage affects communication between the brain and body.

Depending on the injury, it can cause:

- Reduced sensation.
- Muscle weakness.
- Paralysis.
- Changes in bladder and bowel function.

## 3. How does neuroplasticity help after brain injuries?

When certain neural networks are damaged, surviving networks may adapt and help compensate for lost functions.

For example, after a stroke:

1. Brain tissue is damaged.
2. Communication between certain neural networks is disrupted.
3. Surviving networks may reorganize.
4. Rehabilitation provides repeated sensory and motor stimulation.
5. Some abilities may gradually improve.

**Important:** Recovery does not necessarily mean damaged neurons are replaced. Functional improvement can occur through changes in surviving neural networks.

## 4. The role of rehabilitation

!Physical rehabilitation

Rehabilitation uses repeated, purposeful activities to help patients regain or improve functions.

|Type|Main purpose|
|---|---|
|Physical therapy|Movement, balance and coordination|
|Occupational therapy|Everyday activities and independence|
|Speech therapy|Communication and swallowing|
|Cognitive rehabilitation|Memory, attention and problem-solving|

### Example: Recovering movement after a stroke

Imagine someone who loses movement in their right arm following a stroke.

Through rehabilitation, they repeatedly practice:

- Reaching for objects.
- Grasping items.
- Lifting their arm.
- Performing everyday tasks.

These repeated activities provide opportunities for the nervous system to adapt and improve functional performance.

## 5. What influences recovery?

Recovery differs between individuals.

Important factors include:

- Location and severity of the injury.
- Age and general health.
- Access to rehabilitation.
- Consistency of therapy.
- Sleep and overall health.
- Emotional and social support.

Early assessment and appropriate rehabilitation are important for improving outcomes.

## 6. Adaptive vs maladaptive neuroplasticity

Not all brain adaptations are beneficial.

**Adaptive neuroplasticity**

- Supports useful functional changes.
- Can contribute to improved movement and communication.
- Helps the brain adapt following injury.

**Maladaptive neuroplasticity**

- Reinforces unwanted or harmful changes.
- Can contribute to chronic pain.
- May contribute to abnormal movement patterns or persistent symptoms.

The goal of rehabilitation is to encourage useful adaptations while addressing harmful ones.

## 7. Neuroplasticity and psychological recovery

Brain injuries can affect more than physical abilities.

They may also affect:

- Emotional regulation.
- Confidence.
- Independence.
- Social relationships.
- Mental well-being.

Psychological support is therefore an important part of rehabilitation.

Developing coping strategies and receiving social support can help individuals adjust to changes following an injury.

This connects to [[Psychological Resilience]].

## 8. Key takeaways

> [!important]
>
> - Brain injuries can damage neurons and disrupt neural communication.
> - Stroke and traumatic brain injury are common causes of neurological impairment.
> - Neuroplasticity allows surviving neural networks to reorganize.
> - Rehabilitation encourages useful adaptations through repeated practice.
> - Physical, occupational, speech, and cognitive therapies target different functions.
> - Recovery varies between individuals.
> - Neuroplasticity can be adaptive or maladaptive.
> - Psychological support is also important during rehabilitation.
