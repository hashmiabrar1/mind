> [!abstract] Overview Sensory and motor pathways allow the brain to receive information from the environment and send instructions to the body.
>
> **Sensory pathways = receiving information.**
>
> **Motor pathways = producing movement and responses.**

## 1. What are sensory pathways?

Sensory pathways carry information from sensory receptors toward the central nervous system (brain and spinal cord).

They allow us to experience:

- Touch and pressure.
- Pain and temperature.
- Vision.
- Hearing.
- Smell and taste.
- Body position and movement (proprioception).

### How do they work?

Imagine touching a hot object:

1. Sensory receptors detect potentially harmful heat.
2. Sensory neurons transmit information toward the spinal cord.
3. The brain processes the sensory information.
4. You become aware of the sensation.

Some protective reflexes are initiated by spinal circuits before the brain fully processes the sensation.

### Important sensory pathways

|Pathway|Main function|
|---|---|
|Dorsal column–medial lemniscus|Fine touch, vibration and conscious proprioception|
|Spinothalamic tract|Pain, temperature and crude touch|
|Spinocerebellar pathways|Proprioceptive information used for coordination|

## 2. What are motor pathways?

Motor pathways transmit instructions from the central nervous system to muscles and glands.

They allow us to:

- Walk.
- Write.
- Speak.
- Pick up objects.
- Maintain posture.
- Coordinate movements.

### How do they work?

Imagine picking up a glass:

1. Your brain plans the movement.
2. Motor areas send signals through descending pathways.
3. Motor neurons activate the necessary muscles.
4. Muscles contract.
5. Your hand reaches for the glass.

### Important motor pathways

**Corticospinal tract**

- Controls many voluntary movements.
- Particularly important for precise movements of the hands and fingers.

**Extrapyramidal motor systems**

- Include networks involving the basal ganglia, brainstem and other structures.
- Help regulate posture, muscle tone and movement.

## 3. How sensory and motor pathways work together

Sensory and motor systems constantly communicate.

For example, when learning to ride a bicycle:

- Sensory pathways provide information about balance and body position.
- The brain processes the information.
- Motor pathways activate muscles to maintain balance.
- Additional sensory feedback helps correct your movements.

This continuous feedback allows the body to adjust its actions.

## 4. Proprioception: Understanding body position

Proprioception is the ability to sense the position and movement of your body without needing to look at it.

For example:

- Touching your nose with your eyes closed.
- Walking without looking at your feet.
- Knowing where your arm is positioned.

Proprioceptive information comes from receptors in muscles, tendons and joints.

It is essential for balance, coordination and controlled movement.

## 5. How neuroplasticity relates to sensory and motor pathways

Sensory and motor pathways can adapt through experience and practice.

Examples:

- Learning to play piano improves coordination between sensory and motor systems.
- Practicing a sport helps refine movement patterns.
- Rehabilitation after a stroke can encourage useful adaptations in surviving motor networks.
- Learning Braille can involve changes in sensory processing networks.

These are examples of [[Neuroplasticity]].

## 6. Key takeaways

> [!important]
>
> - Sensory pathways carry information toward the central nervous system.
> - Motor pathways carry instructions toward muscles and glands.
> - Sensory and motor systems work together through continuous feedback.
> - Proprioception helps us understand body position and movement.
> - Practice and experience can modify sensory and motor networks.
