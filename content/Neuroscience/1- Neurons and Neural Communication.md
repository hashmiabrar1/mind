> [!abstract] Overview Neurons are specialized nerve cells that receive, process, and transmit information throughout the brain and body. They allow us to think, feel, move, learn, and respond to our environment.

> **Overview**
>
> Neurons are specialized nerve cells that receive, process, and transmit information throughout the brain and body. They allow us to think, feel, move, learn, and respond to our environment.

## 1. What is a neuron?

A neuron is the basic functional unit of the nervous system.

Neurons communicate using electrical and chemical signals and form interconnected networks.

## 2. Structure of a neuron

![[Pasted image 20261121104908.png|503]]

- **Dendrites:** Receive information from other neurons.
- **Cell body (soma):** Contains the nucleus and maintains the neuron.
- **Axon:** Carries electrical impulses away from the cell body.
- **Myelin sheath:** Insulates the axon and helps signals travel faster.
- **Axon terminals:** Release neurotransmitters to communicate with other cells.

**Information flow:**

Dendrites → Cell body → Axon → Axon terminals

## 3. How do neurons communicate?

Neurons use two types of signaling:

1. **Electrical signaling:** An action potential travels along the axon.
2. **Chemical signaling:** Neurotransmitters are released to communicate with the next cell.

### What is a synapse?

!Chemical synapse

A synapse is the junction where one neuron communicates with another cell.

- Presynaptic neuron: Sends the signal.
- Synaptic cleft: Small gap between cells.
- Postsynaptic cell: Receives the signal.

Synapses are important because changes in their strength contribute to learning and memory.

## 4. Important neurotransmitters

|Neurotransmitter|Main function|
|---|---|
|Dopamine|Motivation, reward and movement|
|Serotonin|Mood and sleep|
|GABA|Inhibitory signaling|
|Glutamate|Excitatory signaling and learning|
|Acetylcholine|Muscle activation and memory|
|Norepinephrine|Alertness and stress responses|

## 5. Types of neurons

|Type|Function|
|---|---|
|Sensory neurons|Carry information from sensory receptors to the CNS|
|Motor neurons|Carry signals from the CNS to muscles and glands|
|Interneurons|Connect neurons and process information within the CNS|

### Real-life example: Touching something hot

1. Sensory receptors detect heat.
2. Sensory neurons transmit information toward the spinal cord.
3. Spinal circuits initiate a withdrawal reflex.
4. Motor neurons activate muscles.
5. Your hand moves away.

## 6. Key takeaways

> [!important]
>
> - Neurons are the communication units of the nervous system.
> - Dendrites receive information; axons transmit it.
> - Synapses allow neurons to communicate.
> - Neurotransmitters are chemical messengers.
> - Different neurons perform sensory, motor, and processing functions.
> - Changes in neural connections are central to [[Neuroplasticity]].
