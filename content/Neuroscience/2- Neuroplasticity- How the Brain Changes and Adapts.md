> [!abstract] Overview Neuroplasticity is the brain's ability to change and reorganize its structure, connections, and functioning in response to experiences, learning, and injuries.
>
> In simple terms: **The brain changes according to what we repeatedly learn, experience, and practice.**

## 1. How does neuroplasticity work?

Neurons communicate through connections called synapses.

When we learn or repeatedly practice something, these connections can change.

Important mechanisms include:

- **Long-term potentiation (LTP):** Strengthening of communication between neurons.
- **Long-term depression (LTD):** Weakening of communication between neurons.
- **Structural plasticity:** Physical changes in neural connections and structures.
- **Neurogenesis:** Formation of new neurons.

## 2. Different types of neuroplasticity

|Type|Meaning|Example|
|---|---|---|
|Developmental|Changes during brain development|Childhood language learning|
|Activity-dependent|Changes through repeated experiences|Learning piano|
|Cross-modal|One sensory system takes on functions associated with another|Sensory adaptation in blindness|
|Post-lesional|Changes following injury|Recovery after stroke|
|Maladaptive|Changes that reinforce harmful patterns|Addiction and chronic pain|

## 3. How does learning change the brain?

Learning involves changes in neural connections and networks.

**Example: Learning a new language**

Your brain needs to:

1. Recognize unfamiliar sounds.
2. Understand words and grammar.
3. Remember vocabulary.
4. Coordinate pronunciation.
5. Retrieve information when speaking.

With repeated practice, the neural networks involved can become more efficient.

**Example: Learning to play an instrument**

Motor, auditory, visual, and memory networks work together. Repeated practice helps improve coordination and musical performance.

## 4. Neuroplasticity and nervousness

Imagine giving a presentation in front of 100 people.

You might experience:

- Increased heart rate.
- Sweating.
- Shaking.
- Difficulty concentrating.

The amygdala contributes to processing potential threats, while the prefrontal cortex helps with planning and emotional regulation.

### How can practice help?

Gradually exposing yourself to public speaking can help you learn that the situation is manageable.

For example:

Mirror practice → Speaking to a friend → Small group → Large audience.

Repeated experiences can modify learned fear responses.

This is an example of how neuroplasticity contributes to adapting to unfamiliar situations.

## 5. How can we support healthy neuroplasticity?

|Factor|Potential benefit|
|---|---|
|Physical exercise|Supports brain health and BDNF|
|Learning new skills|Stimulates neural networks|
|Repetition|Reinforces learned skills|
|Sleep|Supports memory consolidation|
|Mindfulness|Supports attention and emotional regulation|
|Social interaction|Engages communication and emotional networks|

**BDNF (brain-derived neurotrophic factor)** is a protein involved in neuronal survival, growth, and synaptic plasticity.

## 6. What can negatively affect brain functioning?

- Chronic stress.
- Insufficient sleep.
- Physical inactivity.
- Substance misuse.
- Neurological injuries.

These factors can interfere with healthy brain functioning. Neuroplasticity can also reinforce harmful patterns, known as maladaptive plasticity.

## 7. Neuroplasticity and brain injuries

!Brain injury and rehabilitation

Following a stroke, certain neural networks may become damaged.

The brain can sometimes reorganize surviving networks to compensate for impaired functions.

Rehabilitation encourages useful adaptations through repeated practice.

Examples:

- Physical therapy: Movement and coordination.
- Occupational therapy: Everyday activities.
- Speech therapy: Communication.
- Cognitive rehabilitation: Memory and attention.

**Important:** Neuroplasticity can support recovery, but it does not guarantee complete recovery.

## 8. Neuroplasticity and psychological resilience

Psychological resilience is the ability to adapt and cope with difficult experiences.

Neuroplasticity can contribute to resilience by allowing changes in neural networks involved in:

- Emotional regulation.
- Memory.
- Stress responses.
- Learning and adaptation.

For example, repeatedly practicing healthier coping strategies can help a person develop more effective responses to stressful situations.

**Remember:** Neuroplasticity is a biological process; resilience is a psychological capacity. They are related but not identical.

## 9. Key takeaways

> [!important]
>
> - Neuroplasticity is the brain's ability to change and reorganize.
> - LTP strengthens synaptic connections; LTD weakens them.
> - Learning and repetition can modify neural networks.
> - Exercise, sleep, and new experiences support brain health.
> - Neuroplasticity can be adaptive or maladaptive.
> - It plays an important role in learning and rehabilitation.
> - It can contribute to psychological resilience.
