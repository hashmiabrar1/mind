> [!abstract] Overview Learning is the process of acquiring new knowledge or skills, while memory allows us to retain and retrieve that information.
>
> Both processes depend on communication and changes within neural networks and are closely connected to [[Neuroplasticity]].

## 1. What is learning?

Learning occurs when we acquire new information, knowledge, or abilities through experience and practice.

Examples:

- Learning a new language.
- Studying biology.
- Learning to drive.
- Developing a musical skill.

When we learn, neural connections can change and become more efficient.

## 2. What is memory?

Memory is the ability to encode, store, and retrieve information.

It allows us to remember:

- Facts and information.
- Personal experiences.
- Skills and habits.
- People and places.

### Three main stages of memory

**Example:** When studying for an exam:

- Encoding: Understanding what you read.
- Storage: Retaining the information.
- Retrieval: Remembering it during the exam.

## 3. Different types of memory

### A. Short-term and working memory

**Short-term memory**

- Holds a limited amount of information for a brief period.

**Working memory**

- Temporarily holds and manipulates information while performing tasks.

Example: Remembering a phone number long enough to enter it, or mentally calculating a sum.

### B. Long-term memory

Stores information over extended periods.

It has two important categories:

**Explicit (declarative) memory**

Information we consciously remember.

- Episodic memory: Personal experiences and events.
- Semantic memory: Facts and general knowledge.

**Implicit (non-declarative) memory**

Learning that does not necessarily require conscious recollection.

- Procedural memory: Skills and habits.
- Conditioning: Learned associations and responses.

Example: Remembering your first day at university is episodic memory, while knowing how to ride a bicycle involves procedural memory.

## 4. Which brain regions are involved?

![[Pasted image 20261121105735.png|370]]
Human brain with highlighted structures

Different brain regions contribute to learning and memory.

|Brain region|Main function|
|---|---|
|Hippocampus|Formation of new declarative memories|
|Prefrontal cortex|Working memory and planning|
|Amygdala|Emotional processing and emotional learning|
|Cerebellum|Motor learning and coordination|
|Basal ganglia|Habits and procedural learning|

**The hippocampus is particularly important:** It helps form new memories, which can become represented in distributed networks across the brain.

## 5. How does neuroplasticity support learning and memory?

Learning involves changes in how neurons communicate.

Two important mechanisms are:

- **Long-term potentiation (LTP):** Strengthening of synaptic connections.
- **Long-term depression (LTD):** Weakening of synaptic connections.

These processes help modify neural networks and support learning and memory.

### Example: Studying a new subject

1. You encounter unfamiliar information.
2. Your brain processes the information.
3. Attention and repetition help reinforce learning.
4. Neural connections can change.
5. With practice and retrieval, the information becomes easier to remember.

This is an example of [[Neuroplasticity]] in action.

## 6. Why is sleep important for memory?

Sleep plays an important role in memory consolidation.

Memory consolidation refers to processes that help stabilize and strengthen newly acquired information.

During sleep:

- The brain processes information acquired during the day.
- Memory representations are reorganized.
- Different brain networks interact to support memory consolidation.

This is one reason why adequate sleep is important when studying.

## 7. How can we improve learning and memory?

|Strategy|Why it helps|
|---|---|
|Active recall|Strengthens retrieval practice|
|Spaced repetition|Reinforces learning over time|
|Adequate sleep|Supports memory consolidation|
|Practice|Helps develop and refine skills|
|Exercise|Supports brain health and cognition|
|Understanding concepts|Helps connect new information to existing knowledge|

**Important:** Re-reading information repeatedly is not always the most effective strategy. Testing yourself and revisiting information over spaced intervals can improve long-term retention.

## 8. Key takeaways

> [!important]
>
> - Learning involves acquiring new knowledge and skills.
> - Memory allows information to be encoded, stored and retrieved.
> - The hippocampus is important for forming new declarative memories.
> - The prefrontal cortex supports working memory.
> - Different memory systems involve different brain networks.
> - LTP and LTD contribute to learning-related synaptic changes.
> - Sleep supports memory consolidation.
> - Learning and memory are closely connected to [[Neuroplasticity]].
